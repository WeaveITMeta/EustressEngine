## 1. What can and cannot be enforced

This decides the shape of everything else.

| Kind of play | Where it runs | What a server can enforce |
|---|---|---|
| Browser, a world that runs on the visitor's machine (version 1) | The visitor's browser | How many worlds a guest is allowed to start, and a time lease the server measures with its own clock. Not what a modified browser does with content it already downloaded. |
| A world hosted by another user's Studio | The host's machine | Nothing today. `GET /api/simulations/{id}/live` returns the join link, with its key and pin, to anyone for an approved listing, and the host checks nothing. A guest needs no ticket to join. |

So the guest allowance is a **funnel and capacity limit, not content protection**. Published worlds are public by design: the desktop Player downloads them anonymously. Version 1 therefore covers the first row only, and says so to McKale. Hosted guest play waits until the join link is gated and hosts check who joins (section 8).

## 2. Who is a guest

**McKale's rule: one guest allowance per network address per day.** So the guest is the address, and there is nothing for the visitor to store.

- The Worker derives the guest id from the caller's address: `gid = HMAC(day key, address)`, with IPv6 reduced to its /64 and IPv4-mapped IPv6 read as IPv4. The day key is `HMAC(JWT_SECRET, "eustress-guest-day:" + UTC date)`, so the id changes every UTC day, cannot be turned back into the address, and cannot be matched from one day to the next.
- No cookie, no `localStorage` token, no header. Clearing storage changes nothing, since the address is the identity.
- Everyone behind one address shares one allowance: a household, a school, an office, a phone carrier. The first visitor of the day may play as a guest, and the next one sees the register prompt. McKale accepted this. A VPN gets a new address and so a new allowance, which he also accepted as known.
- Nothing about the visitor is recorded except the chosen character, `M` or `F`, inside the ticket. The page may remember that choice in the tab (`sessionStorage`) to pre-fill the avatar if the visitor registers.

## 3. The allowance

One Durable Object per guest id (which already contains the day), holding:

- `sims`: the distinct simulation ids started that day, at most **3**.
- `seconds`: seconds of play used that day, at most **3600**.
- `lease`: the one active lease, if any (section 4).

It deletes itself by alarm 25 hours after its day began. Starting a simulation already counted that day does not count again.

## 4. Leases: time measured by the server

A **ticket** is permission to hold a lease on one simulation. It is not a countdown the browser reports.

1. `POST /api/guest/play {sim_id, character}`: the Worker checks that the listing is approved and rated `all_ages` (section 6), that the guest has a sim slot and seconds left, and that this guest holds no other lease. It answers `{ticket, expires_in, remaining_seconds, sims_left}`. The ticket is a signed `{typ:"lease", tid, gid, sim, character, day, iat}`, valid for a **full 60-minute lease or the seconds left, whichever is less**, and debited against its issue day, so a guest is never cut off at midnight UTC.
2. The browser Player sends `POST /api/guest/beat {ticket}` every 30 s. Each beat debits the time the Worker measured since the previous beat, **capped at 1.5 times the interval**, and answers `{remaining}` or `{expired}`. No beat for 90 s ends the lease.
3. A second lease on the same guest ends the first: a ticket pasted into a chat serves one player.
4. There is **no refund and no host-reported leave**. A client that stops beating only hurts itself. This closes the colluding-host and the sock-puppet attacks on the previous draft's reserve-and-refund model.

The browser Player stops play when a beat answers `expired`. A modified client can keep running content it already holds, which section 1 accepts.

## 5. Abuse limits

- **One allowance per address per day** (section 2) is the main limit. There is no human check (McKale decided against Turnstile) and no mint cap: there is nothing to mint, since the id is the address.
- The address is never stored. The Durable Object is named by the day-keyed hash and deletes itself by alarm within 25 hours.
- `POST /api/guest/play` also goes through the per-address rate limiter used for the auth routes (`AUTH_RATE_LIMITER`, 30 a minute).
- Known and accepted: a VPN or a changing mobile address gets another allowance. The content is public, so there is little to gain.
- A listing may cap its guest sessions later (a share of `max_players`), so account holders always get in. That belongs to hosted play.

## 6. Which simulations

A simulation is guest-playable when `isListable` holds (approved and public) **and** its rating is `all_ages` or `ages_10` (G to PG, McKale 2026-10-16), **and** it runs in the browser (section 9) or the guest uses the desktop Player. Guests have no age check and may be children, so anything rated above `ages_10` asks for an account. Guest sessions have no voice chat, text chat checked before delivery (section 13), a fixed display name "Guest", and persist nothing.

## 7. The pages

1. **Play** on a listing, signed out: a dialog titled "Choose your character" with two buttons, "Male" and "Female". The listing's own page already shows OFFLINE or LIVE and the rating.
2. The choice calls `/api/guest/play`. A refusal carries `{code: "guest_limit", reason: "sims" | "minutes", resets_at}` and the page shows what ran out and when it resets (midnight UTC, shown in the visitor's local time), with a Register button.
3. After registering, the page returns to the listing and offers Play with the account: the account's launch needs no guest ticket, and accounts have no guest allowance. The `M` or `F` choice, remembered in the tab, becomes the new account's starting avatar (`PUT /api/avatar`).
4. Superseded on 2026-10-16: the desktop Player plays as a guest too (section 13). Signed out, it already plays solo today ("Play on your own without an account" on the download page), because published worlds download anonymously.

## 8. Guests on hosted sessions (Worker built 2026-10-16, not deployed)

The three prerequisites are in place: `GET /live` gives the link only to an account or a live guest lease (HOSTED_ADMISSION.md); the host registers each join at `POST /live/admit`, single use per hosting session; and the host already treats `guest: true` as a guest (guest user ids 2^52 + peer, no purchases, its chat dropped: networking `session.rs`, engine `multiplayer.rs` `ask_gallery`, `remote_players.rs`).

**The flow.**

1. The guest opens a lease: `POST /api/guest/play {sim_id, character, client}`. `client` is `"browser"` (the default, which also needs `browser_play` not false) or `"player"` (the desktop Player, which runs any listing). Same allowance, same all-ages rule.
2. The guest reads the link: `GET /api/simulations/{id}/live` or `POST /api/simulations/{id}/play` with `X-Eustress-Lease: <lease>`. The lease must be on THAT listing (new on 2026-10-16: a lease on listing A no longer reveals listing B's host).
3. The guest mints a join ticket: `POST /api/guest/ticket {lease, audience: <pin from the link>}`. Rate limited per address like the auth routes. Refusals: 410 `bad_ticket` (not a lease), 400 `bad_audience`, 410 `lease_ended`, 404 (listing gone, private or not approved), 403 `guest_not_allowed` (no longer `all_ages`), 409 `not_live` (nobody hosts the listing at that pin). 200: `{ticket, id, expires, guest: true, name: "Guest"}`.
   - The ticket is `egt1.<payload>.<mac>`, payload `{j: id, h: pin, s: listing, e: expiry}`, 10 minutes, HMAC under a key derived from `JWT_SECRET` with the label `eustress-guest-join-v1`. Nothing about the person is in it, not even the guest id or the lease: those are held by the guest-only session's object under `j` (section 14, G-3). Minting needs `GUEST_PLAY` on and a guest-only session at that pin.
4. The Player sends it as its Hello ticket; the host asks `POST /live/admit` as it does for accounts. For a guest ticket the route checks, before the Durable Object spends the ticket: it is genuine, unexpired and for this pin; it names THIS listing (else `wrong_host`); the listing is `all_ages` and not adult (else 403 `guest_not_allowed`); its lease is still beating (else 403 `lease_ended`). The listing must serve, as for every joiner (else `not_available`). The Durable Object spends it once (`used` on reuse), refuses past `MAX_GUESTS` = 100 guests in one session (`not_available`), and answers `{ok, id, account_id: null, username: "Guest", guest: true, expires}`.
5. While the host names the guest's ticket id in its heartbeat's `admitted` list, each heartbeat beats the guest's lease (the same debit as a guest's own beat, measured by the Worker's clock, capped per beat). A guest the host stops naming is forgotten after 90 s, and its lease then ends by its own timeout. The heartbeat's answer carries `ended_guests: [ids]` when a connected guest's lease has ended (time used up, a newer lease from the same address, or ended by the guest); it repeats on every beat until the host stops naming that id, and is absent otherwise, so a session without guests answers exactly as before.
6. The host stopping, or a new pin (a restart), forgets every guest of the session.

**Guest join tickets are never accounts.** Another prefix and another key from the identity ticket (`eit1`) and the session token. `/api/identity/verify` refuses one (401), so the host's `remote_players.rs` plays that peer as a guest; no route accepts one as a bearer token. Tests: `infrastructure/cloudflare/api/tests/guest_join.test.mjs`.

## 13. Plan: the host, the Player and the site (not built)

Hosted guest play is OFF in the Worker until two things are true: the deployment sets `GUEST_PLAY = "on"` (wrangler.toml ships `"off"` for production and staging; unset, or any value but exactly `on`, is off), and the host's latest heartbeat declares the guest protections. Solo guest play (a lease, alone) does not read the setting.

**Guest-only sessions (McKale, 2026-10-16).** Guests get their own servers. A host runs a session either for accounts (today's) or for guests (`guest_only: true` on every heartbeat), never both in one session. The Worker holds the two side by side: the account session in the LiveHost object named by the listing id, the guest-only session in the object `guest:<listing id>`. A guest ticket is minted only for a guest-only session's pin and admitted only by it (`account_session` if an account session is asked to); an account ticket is refused by a guest-only session (`guest_only_session`). Guest-only sessions are listed in their own index (`GET /api/guest/live`, the same shape as `/api/simulations/live`, 503 while `GUEST_PLAY` is off) and never on the Gallery's strip. A session is listed only while a guest could join it: `GUEST_PLAY` on, the host's latest heartbeat declaring all four guest protections, the host's chat not refused 3 times that day, and the listing guest-eligible (`guestEligible`: approved and serving, public, G to PG, not adult); a heartbeat that fails any of these takes it out of the index at once. A guest holding a lease reads `GET /live` and `POST /play` about the guest-only session only (the answer carries `guest_only: true`), and only while `GUEST_PLAY` is on: with it off, a lease is answered as any signed-out visitor is (no link).

**One pin, one kind of session (round 3, R-1).** A certificate pin is one host process, so the two sessions of one listing never share a pin. A heartbeat at the pin the listing's other session is live at is refused 409 `pin_in_use` (a guest-only one also says `end_all_guests: true`); `POST /api/guest/ticket` refuses a pin the account session is live at (409 `pin_in_use`); `/live/admit` refuses any ticket whose pin the other session is live at (403 `pin_in_use`). The two sessions live in two Durable Objects, so a heartbeat racing another heartbeat can slip past for one beat; the next beat of either is refused.

**Multiplayer (host, engine `multiplayer.rs` and networking `session.rs`). The exact changes the Worker now requires (G-1).** Until all of this ships and the host declares it, the Worker admits no guest to that host.

1. **The capability field.** A guest-only host sends, on every gallery heartbeat (`POST /api/simulations/{id}/live`), `"guest_only": true` and `"guest_caps": ["no_guest_chat", "guest_name", "ended_guests", "host_chat_checked"]` (live.mjs `GUEST_CAPS_REQUIRED`; order free, at most 16 names of `[a-z_]{1,32}`, anything else is 400 `bad_guest_caps`). Admission reads the host's LATEST heartbeat: a beat without all four makes `/live/admit` refuse every guest (`guest_not_supported`), ends every connected guest on that beat, and takes the session out of the guest index. The host declares a capability only when the build enforces it, so an older host can never take a guest. Its `/live/admit` call carries `"guest_only": true` too, and its stop is `DELETE /live?guest_only=true`. The guest-only session runs in its own host process with its own certificate: never at the pin of the listing's account session (section 13, "One pin").
2. **`no_guest_chat`: chat both ways goes through the check.** The original requirement was no chat to or from guests. McKale's 2026-10-16 decision allows guest chat, moderated before delivery, so the capability now means: (a) no chat line from any guest reaches anyone until the Worker answers `deliver: true` for it (below); (b) the host's own regex filter runs first and masks matches with symbols (for example `$#%*`); (c) chat from accounts never reaches a guest-only session, since it has no accounts; (d) between guests, every line is checked, whichever guest sent it. In code: in `session.rs`, where `ToHost::Chat` is relayed (`host.ready()`), a guest peer's line is held, masked, sent to `POST /api/guest/chat/check`, and relayed only on `deliver: true`; any other answer, a timeout or a network error drops it (fail closed).
3. **`guest_name`: the name "Guest".** Every guest peer is shown as `Guest` (or `Guest N` to tell peers apart), whatever its Hello carries. The Hello name is never stored, shown, logged or relayed. Today (`session.rs:2215`) the host takes the Hello name.
4. **`ended_guests`: honoured at once, and every guest let go when the Worker cannot be heard.** Read `ended_guests` from every heartbeat's 200, and from a chat check's `action: "kick"` (`end_guest: true`), and disconnect each named peer whose `admitted` id matches, with the notice "Your guest time has ended. Sign in to keep playing." (for a refused chat line: "You were removed from this session."). Disconnect EVERY guest when any answer carries `end_all_guests: true` (a heartbeat refused 401, 403 or 404, a guest-only heartbeat refused 409 `pin_in_use`, a heartbeat or chat check after the host's own chat was refused 3 times that day), and when 90 s pass without a heartbeat answered 200 (a 5xx, a timeout, no network: the Worker's seats for those guests are gone by then, and any id the host names without a seat is reported ended on the next beat). Today (`session.rs:2766`) the host ignores all of it.
5. A guest-only host keeps naming its connected guests' ticket ids in `admitted` on every beat (the grace for a missed name is 90 s from the last beat that named it). An id it names that holds no seat in the Worker (after a heartbeat gap past 90 s, or a Worker restart of the session) is reported in `ended_guests`.
6. **`host_chat_checked`: the host's own lines go through the check.** A guest-only session's host (the listing's author, playing in it) sends every chat line of its own that would reach a guest to `POST /api/guest/chat/check {sim_id, from: "host", text}` and delivers it only on `action: "deliver"`; any other answer, a timeout or a network error drops it (fail closed). A refused host line is dropped and kicks nobody. The 3rd refusal of the host's lines in one UTC day (`HOST_CHAT_REFUSALS_MAX` = 3, guest.mjs) answers `end_all_guests: true`, every later heartbeat that day answers it too and ends every guest, and the session takes no guest (`host_chat_barred` at `/api/guest/ticket` and `/live/admit`) and is not listed until midnight UTC. A restart (a new pin) does not clear it.
7. Tests: a mutation-checked host test for each of 1 to 6.

**The guest chat check (Worker, built; round 3).** `POST /api/guest/chat/check`, the host's own session (FRESH, the listing's author), `GUEST_PLAY` on. Two kinds of line:

- a guest's: `{sim_id, id: <the guest's join ticket id>, text}`; the seat must be connected to that listing's guest-only session (else 404 `not_connected`);
- the host's own, to its guests: `{sim_id, from: "host", text}` (no `id`); the listing's guest-only session must be live (else 404 `not_live`), and not barred for the day (else 403 `host_chat_barred` with `end_all_guests: true`).

In order: a rate limit per seat, and one for the host's own lines (`GUEST_CHAT_RATE_LIMITER`, 20 a minute per key, `<sim>:<id>` or `<sim>:host`; unbound fails closed: 503 `check_unavailable`); then the Worker's own rules (`src/guest_chat_rules.mjs`, below), which cost nothing; then the day's caps (below); then Jev through moderation.mjs `checkCommunityText({ text, field: 'forum_post', env })`, the forum battery (CSAM, non-consensual sexual content, personal information as doxxing, threats, scams, spam, adult sexual content), with the review-cost run record turned off for that call (section 16).

The host is told one of three actions and nothing more: never the category or the rule.

| Answer | Status | When | What the host does |
|---|---|---|---|
| `{deliver: true, action: "deliver"}` | 200 | Jev passes the line | deliver it |
| `{deliver: false, action: "block", code: "empty"}` | 200 | nothing but spaces and invisible characters (or Jev's own shape refusal: empty once cleaned) | drop it; nobody leaves |
| `{deliver: false, action: "kick", code: "refused", end_guest: true, ended_guests: [id]}` | 200 | a guest's line refused by the rules (personal information) or by Jev (any category, or adult content) | drop it and disconnect that guest |
| `{deliver: false, action: "block", code: "refused"}` (plus `end_all_guests: true` on the 3rd that day) | 200 | the host's own line refused, the same way | drop it; on `end_all_guests`, disconnect every guest |
| `{deliver: false, action: "block", code: "check_unavailable"}` | 503 | Jev down, not configured or timed out; a day's cap reached; the cap count unreachable | drop it; nobody leaves |
| `{deliver: false, action: "block", code: "rate_limited"}` | 429 | over the rate limit | drop it |
| `400 bad_request`, `400 too_long` (above 500 characters), `401`, `403 not_author` | | | drop it |

After a kick the seat is ended (in `ended_guests` from the next heartbeat on), the lease ends, and the guest is barred from that listing for the rest of the UTC day it is refused on: in its allowance (`barred`, `guest_barred` at `/api/guest/play`) for the lease's guest id and, when the lease was opened before midnight and the line is refused after it, for the same address's guest id of the new day as well (held in the allowance as `next`, section 16); and in the session object by lease (`gbar:<tid>`) and by guest id (`gbarg:<gid>`), so `/api/guest/ticket` and `/live/admit` refuse it (`guest_barred`). A bar the allowance cannot write is tried twice at once, then queued (`gbarq:<tid>`) and retried on every heartbeat until written; meanwhile the session's guest-id bar keeps a new lease from the same address out of that session.

**The Worker's own rules (`src/guest_chat_rules.mjs`, round 3, B-3).** Deterministic, over the line in NFKC, lowercased, invisible format characters removed. A line matching any of these is a personal-information refusal (a kick), and Jev is not asked: a link (`http`, `https`, `www.`, a word joined by a dot to a common top-level domain such as `site.com` or `emma.k.2015.gg`, the dot also spelled or bracketed: `site dot com`, `site (dot) com`, `site[.]com`, and `dot com|net|org|gg|io` alone); a Discord invite (`discord.gg`, `discord.com/invite`, `discordapp.com/invite`, also spelled or spaced out); an @handle or a Discord tag (`name#1234`); a contact or social network named (discord, snap but not "oh snap", snapchat, insta, instagram, tiktok, telegram, whatsapp, kik, facebook, twitter, skype), or contact asked for or offered ("dm me", "pm me", "text me", "call me", "message me", "my number", "my username", "your snap", "ur discord"); a phone number or an email address (moderation.mjs `profilePersonalInfo`: 7 or more digits with only spaces, dots, brackets or hyphens between them; an address written out or spelled with "at" and "dot"); a street address (a house number, one to three words, a street word: "42 maple street"); a school named as one's own ("I go to / attend ... elementary | middle | high | primary | secondary | junior | academy | school | prep | kindergarten", "my school is") or asked ("what school", "where do you go to school"); a place one lives ("I live in / at / on / near / by", "I'm from ...", "my address", "my house is", "my town is", "my city is", "my street", "my zip") or asked ("where do you live", "where are you from", "what city are you in"). They catch exactly these patterns: a number spelled in words, a handle without an @, an address in another order, or a grooming approach in ordinary words is left to Jev, whose forum battery asks about doxxing, not self-disclosure (the request below). Known misfires, each a kick: a 7-digit score written with spaces, "I'm from the future", "42 lap way".

**Daily caps on chat checks (round 3, B-4).** Each line that reaches Jev is a paid model call. Before it is sent, it is counted against three daily caps, held in one LiveHost object (`guest-chat-caps`, `caps:<UTC day>` = `{total, hosts: {<host account>: n}, sims: {<listing>: n}}`): per listing `GUEST_CHAT_CAP_LISTING` (default 5,000), per host account `GUEST_CHAT_CAP_HOST` (default 5,000), deployment-wide `GUEST_CHAT_CAP_TOTAL` (default 50,000). McKale sets the final numbers in wrangler.toml `[vars]` (commented there until he does; an unset or non-numeric value is the default). Past any cap, or when the count cannot be reached: 503 `check_unavailable`, not delivered, nobody kicked. Lines the rules refuse are never counted. The counts are deleted 25 hours after their day began (the object's alarm). The forum's own per-account cap (`forum_checks_per_account_per_day`) is not used for guest chat, so a busy guest session never uses up its host's forum posting.

No chat text is stored anywhere: the only records are a console line `{event: "guest_chat_check", from: "guest" | "host", outcome, reason}` (`reason` a rule code such as `rule:handle` or Jev's reason code such as `jev:model`, never the text), per-outcome counters `chat_n:<outcome>` in the guest-only session object, the host's refusal count `hostref:<day>`, and the day's cap counts.

## 16. COPPA: every datum on a guest path (for counsel)

Design rule: data minimisation under the support-for-internal-operations exception. The Worker keeps no persistent identifier for a guest: the only per-person keys are hashes keyed to one UTC day. Every record that holds a guest id is deleted, or has the guest id stripped, 25 hours after the guest id's day began (the allowance by its alarm; a session's waiting tickets, seats and bars on its next heartbeat after that time, at most 30 s later, or by its alarm once the host is gone) (since round 3 nothing re-creates a guest id's record after its alarm, R-2, and no waiting ticket outlives it, R-4). The visit markers are keyed by a further hash under the visit key of their own day and expire 25 hours after they are written, so one written late in a day lasts into the day after next; they cannot be matched to a guest id or across days. The Worker stores no chat text. Nothing about a guest reaches a host from the Worker but the name "Guest" and a random per-join ticket id. Nothing is saved for a guest (no progress, purchases, inventory or names). What the Worker cannot promise is listed after the table: the host receives the guest's network address and the guest's chat lines in clear.

**"Filtered" means exactly this.** Before a guest chat line (or the host's line to guests) is delivered, the Worker's own rules (section 13, `src/guest_chat_rules.mjs`) refuse links, Discord invites, @handles and Discord tags, named contact and social networks and asking for or offering contact, phone numbers, email addresses, street addresses ("number, words, street word"), a school named as one's own or asked, and a place one lives stated or asked, in the fixed patterns listed in section 13 and in no other form. Jev's forum battery then judges what is left, and today asks about doxxing (someone's private information shared against their will), not about a child sharing its own details or an adult seeking contact: that is the `guest_chat` request to Jev (section 13). The host's own regex filter runs before both. Personal information written in a form the rules do not list and Jev does not refuse is delivered.

**What the Worker cannot promise, for counsel:**
1. **The host holds the guest's network address and chat lines in clear.** A hosted session is a direct QUIC connection from the guest's Player to the host's machine (another user: the listing's author), so the host receives the guest's IP address at the network layer, and every chat line a guest sends passes through the host in clear before the host asks the Worker whether to deliver it. The Worker discloses nothing beyond "Guest" and a random ticket id, but a hosted guest session is not address-private, and a modified host can read, keep or deliver guest chat without the check. A relay would be needed to change the first; the second is the host's declared capability, checked only by its declaration (section 17).
2. **Workers Logs.** `observability` is on in both environments. Counsel should check it against the 25-hour line, and turn logging off for the guest routes or sample it if needed.
3. **Jev as a processor.** Guest chat lines (and the host's lines to guests) and nothing else about the guest are sent to Jev's endpoint for classification.
4. **Filtering is pattern-based and model-based, not complete.** See "Filtered means exactly this" above.

**A decision for counsel (LEGAL, design only, OFF):** today a guest chat line that Jev refuses as `csam`, `threat` or enticement is neither kept nor reported: the guest is kicked and barred, and the line is gone. The forum path, for the same categories, seals the post in the evidence vault and files it (moderation.mjs `fileForumReport`, legal_reports.mjs, takedown.mjs: Jev's files). The option: when the check refuses a guest's line as `csam` (and, if counsel says so, `threat` or a future `contact_seeking` / enticement category at `confidence_band: 'high'`), seal that one line in the evidence vault with the listing id, the host account id, the session's random ticket id and the time (never the guest id or an address, which the Worker does not have), and file it the way the forum path files a post (an NCMEC CyberTipline report for CSAM; a law-enforcement referral for a credible threat). That keeps illegal content the law may require be preserved and reported, and it is a retention of a child's words that the minimisation design otherwise avoids. Not built: no storage, no report, nothing in guest.mjs reads a vault. Counsel decides whether the reporting duty applies to a line the Worker only classified and never stored, and McKale decides whether to build it.

## 17. Known limits, left as notes (Security round 2)

- **Workers Logs fields:** see section 16, item 2.
- **KV staleness:** listing records are read from KV, which can trail a write by up to about a minute; a re-rating or takedown reaches the guest checks (eligibility at mint, admit and heartbeat) as soon as KV shows it, so a guest can stay up to one heartbeat (30 s) past what KV shows, plus KV's own delay.
- **100 Durable Object calls per heartbeat:** each connected guest's lease is beaten from the host's heartbeat, one GuestAllowance call per guest, at most `MAX_GUESTS` = 100. A guest-only session at the cap costs 100 calls per 30 s beat.
- **Hosted content honesty:** the host is the listing's author and runs the world; the Worker checks the listing (its rating, approval and binding) but cannot check what a modified host actually serves to its guests, or that it enforces what it declares in `guest_caps`. A host that lies is a moderation case against the author's account.
